# Sources for the human-cover-letter skill

Read 13 Oct 2026 unless noted. Strength: Official, Good, Weak. Re-check a rule against its source before changing it.

| Source | What it supports | Read how | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Tahatu (NZ Government careers): using AI to create a CV or cover letter](https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/using-ai-to-create-a-cv-or-cover-letter/), page updated 29 Sep 2026 | Recruiters can often spot AI applications; AI is generic, may not sound like you, may use the wrong NZ spelling; personalise with your own achievements; check tone; get a second reader | In full | Official |
| [Tahatu: how to write a cover letter](https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cover-letter) | Tailor each letter; one page; do not repeat the CV | In full, 13 Oct | Official |
| [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) (WikiProject AI Cleanup) | Patterns in machine text; warnings that humans are poor detectors and that fixing signs is not fixing causes. Read the first 100,000 characters of 254,000 | Partly | Good for patterns; not proof for any one letter |
| [Cui, Dias, Ye, "Signaling in the Age of AI: Evidence from Cover Letters"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25054), v2 12 Nov 2025 | After an AI letter tool launched on one platform, the link between ad-matching and callbacks fell 51%; employers used work history more; more editing time linked to better outcomes | Abstract | Weak: one working paper, one platform (freelance jobs) |
| Galdin and Silbert (Princeton, Dartmouth) and a Journal of Labor Economics field study (Abbas Nejad and others) | Same direction: AI makes letters look better without more interviews; recruiters value human-crafted letters once AI use is known | Search summaries only | Weak: not opened |
| [Paul Graham, "Write Simply"](https://paulgraham.com/simply.html) | Ordinary words, simple sentences, draft fast then cut; plain writing shows when you have nothing to say | In full | Good (an essay, not research) |
| [Orwell, "Politics and the English Language"](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/) | Six rules: no stock figures of speech, short words, cut words, active voice, no jargon | Rules and section summaries | Good (classic, opinion) |
| Career-site pieces on cover letters (Zety, Fortune, Grammarly, Illinois graduate college; found through a search) | Open with something specific, tell one story, avoid generic openers and buzzwords | Search summary only | Weak: vendors, conventions |
| Length pieces (Resume Genius, Enhancv and others) | 250 to 400 words, one page, 30 to 60 second read | Search summary only | Weak: vendors, figures unsourced |
| [Trade Me, how to judge an application in a ChatGPT world](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/employer/article/how-to-judge-a-job-application-in-a-chatgpt-world), 21 Aug 2026 | Only headings loaded; one principle: "AI should be an assistant rather than a fabricator" | Headings only | Weak |
| Ask a Manager (Alison Green) cover-letter posts | Search snippets say: a letter should say what makes you well suited beyond the résumé. The site blocked automated page loads (403), so the posts were not read | Snippets only | Weak: not read |

Unresolved: whether a bulleted letter helps or hurts. An older SEEK NZ piece (2020, two recruiters) liked scannable bullets. Wikipedia lists bold-label bullets as a machine sign. Default here is prose, and one short list only when the reader is clearly a screener.
