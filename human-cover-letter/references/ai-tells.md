# Patterns that make a letter sound machine-made

Use as a prompt to look, not proof. Humans use some of these too. The cure is true specific content, not swapping words. Source tags: W = Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" (read 13 Oct 2026); T = NZ Tahatu AI page (updated 29 Sep 2026); I = my reading, `inferred`.

| Pattern | Looks like | Plain replacement | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stock opener | "I am writing to express my interest...", "I am excited/thrilled to apply" | Start with the role and your real reason in your own words | T, I |
| Empty enthusiasm | "passionate", "driven", "results-oriented", "dynamic" | Show one thing you did. Cut the adjective | I |
| Puffed-up importance | "pivotal", "crucial", "testament to", "plays a vital role", "stands as" | Say what it was and what happened | W |
| Marketing verbs | "boasts", "features", "offers", "leverage", "spearhead", "showcase", "foster", "cutting-edge", "robust", "seamless" | has, uses, led, built, ran | W, I |
| Avoiding "is" | "serves as", "functions as", "acts as" | is | W |
| Not-just-but | "not just using an assistant but shaping it", "more than X, it's Y" | Say the positive claim once | W |
| Lists of three | "fast, reliable and scalable", "design, build and ship" | Keep the one that is true and proven | W, I |
| -ing tails | "..., highlighting my commitment to quality", "..., underscoring the value of testing" | End the sentence at the fact | W |
| Vague praise of the employer | "your commitment to excellence", "your innovative approach" | Quote one concrete thing from the ad or product | W, I |
| Vague attribution | "industry experts agree", "it is widely known" | Name the source or cut | W |
| Matching headers | "Against your list:", one bold label per ad requirement | Prose paragraphs; echo the ad's words inside sentences | W, I |
| Bold-label bullets | "- **Workflow.** I write the spec..." | Plain sentences | W |
| Em dashes | "..., and then, after a pause, the result" | Full stop or comma | W |
| Wrap-up sentence | "In summary...", "Overall, my experience aligns with your needs" | Cut. End on the concrete next step | I |
| Closing boilerplate | "I look forward to the opportunity to discuss how my skills align..." | "Happy to show you the demo", "I can talk any afternoon" | I |
| Even rhythm | Every sentence 20 to 28 words, every paragraph the same shape | Mix very short and medium sentences | I |
| Polished with no person | No mistake, no choice, no "I noticed", no limit | Add the true moment, the trade-off, the gap | T, I |
| Wrong locale | American spelling, "resume" where the ad says CV, "Dear Hiring Manager" in NZ | Local spelling; "Kia ora" or a name | T |
| Leftover template text | "[Company Name]", "[YOUR EXAMPLE]", curly-quote leftovers | Fill or cut before sending | W |

## How to test a flagged line

1. Is it true and specific? Keep it, but trim it.
2. Could it be said about any employer? Cut it or add the detail.
3. Would the person say it aloud to a friend? If not, rewrite in their words.
